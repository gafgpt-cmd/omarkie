# Midnight Lemon Olive Oil Cake :lemon:

**One bowl. No mixer. Bright as a summer morning.**

<img src="images/lemon-cake.jpg" alt="A lemon olive oil cake with white glaze on a plate, with lemons beside it" width="560">

This is the cake you bake when friends say they will "drop by for a minute". It is soft, a little golden at the edges, and it tastes even better on day two. *If* there is a day two.

## What you need

| Ingredient | Amount | Note |
| :--------- | -----: | :--- |
| Plain flour | 200 g | sifted, if you have time |
| Sugar | 180 g | caster sugar is ideal |
| Eggs | 3 | at room temperature |
| Olive oil | 150 ml | a mild, fruity one |
| Plain yoghurt | 120 g | full fat |
| Lemons | 2 | zest **and** juice |
| Baking powder | 2 tsp | |
| Salt | a pinch | do not skip it |

## Method

- [x] Heat the oven to 180 °C and line a 23 cm tin
- [x] Rub the lemon zest into the sugar with your fingers until it smells amazing
- [ ] Whisk in the eggs, then the oil, yoghurt and juice
- [ ] Fold in the flour, baking powder and salt. ==Stop as soon as it looks smooth.==
- [ ] Bake for 40 to 45 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean
- [ ] Cool for 15 minutes before you add the glaze

> [!WARNING]
> Do not open the oven in the first 30 minutes. The middle will sink, and you will be sad.

## The glaze

Mix 100 g icing sugar with 2 tbsp lemon juice. It should run off the spoon *slowly*, like honey. Pour it over the warm cake and let it drip down the sides.

~~Wait until it cools completely~~ Eat one slice warm. Nobody will tell.

## Baker's notes

1. Swap one lemon for an orange in winter. :tangerine:
2. A handful of blueberries makes it a party. :blueberries:
3. Sea salt flakes on top are an excellent idea.

## Kitchen maths

Scaling for a crowd? Multiply every amount by the same factor *k*. For a 28 cm tin instead of 23 cm, the area grows by

$$
k = \left(\frac{28}{23}\right)^2 \approx 1.48
$$

So you need about ==one and a half times== the batter. Baking time goes up by roughly 10 minutes.

Oven conversions: 180 °C is about 356 °F, or gas mark 4. Water boils at 100 °C at sea level, because H~2~O is stubborn like that.
